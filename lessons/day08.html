Professional Email Etiquette & Business Writing

Duration: 60-90 min | 21 Modules | Tier: Intermediate | Grammar: Formal Sentence Structures & Connectors

Module 1: Daily Motivation

"Clear communication is the bridge between confusion and clarity."

Welcome to Day 8! Today we focus on how to write clear, professional, and impactful emails that get results.

Module 2: Warm-up Conversation

Answer the following 5 questions to warm up.

1. How many emails do you send daily?

2. What makes an email professional?

3. Have you ever misunderstood an email?

4. Do you use email templates?

5. What is the hardest part about writing business emails?

Module 3: Vocabulary

Correspondence

Definition: Communication by exchanging letters or emails.

Salutation

Definition: A standard formula of words used in a letter to address the person being written to.

Recipient

Definition: A person who receives something.

Attachment

Definition: A computer file sent along with an email.

Agenda

Definition: A list of items to be discussed at a formal meeting.

Pursuant

Definition: In accordance with (a law or a legal document or resolution).

Hereby

Definition: As a result of this document or utterance.

Aforementioned

Definition: Denoting a thing or person previously mentioned.

Concisely

Definition: In a way that is brief but comprehensive.

Formally

Definition: In accordance with the rules of convention or etiquette.

Professionally

Definition: In a way that relates to a person's work or career.

Acknowledge

Definition: Accept or admit the existence or truth of.

Confirm

Definition: Establish the truth or correctness of.

Clarify

Definition: Make a statement or situation less confused and more comprehensible.

Reiterate

Definition: Say something again or a number of times, typically for emphasis or clarity.

Summarize

Definition: Give a brief statement of the main points of something.

Enumerate

Definition: Mention a number of things one by one.

Ascertain

Definition: Find something out for certain; make sure of.

Facilitate

Definition: Make an action or process easy or easier.

Coordinate

Definition: Bring the different elements of a complex activity or organization into a relationship that will ensure efficiency or harmony.

Expedite

Definition: Make an action or process happen sooner or be accomplished more quickly.

Notify

Definition: Inform someone of something, typically in a formal or official manner.

Convey

Definition: Make an idea, impression, or feeling known or understandable to someone.

Dispatch

Definition: Send off to a destination or for a purpose.

Transmit

Definition: Cause something to pass on from one place or person to another.

Module 4: Phrasal Verbs

Follow up on

Meaning: To investigate or track the progress of something.

Get back to

Meaning: To respond to someone later.

Copy in

Meaning: To include someone in an email (CC).

Loop in

Meaning: To bring someone into a communication thread.

Touch base

Meaning: To briefly connect with someone.

Circle back

Meaning: To discuss something again later.

Reach out to

Meaning: To contact someone, often for the first time or after a while.

Wrap up

Meaning: To conclude a meeting or email.

Sign off

Meaning: To end an email formally.

Fill in

Meaning: To provide missing information to someone.

Module 5: Idioms

Get the ball rolling

Meaning: To start a process.

Put something in writing

Meaning: To document a verbal agreement.

Cover your tracks

Meaning: To protect oneself by documenting actions.

Above board

Meaning: Legitimate, honest, and open.

Read between the lines

Meaning: To understand a hidden meaning.

Keep in the loop

Meaning: To keep someone informed.

On the same page

Meaning: To have a shared understanding.

In a nutshell

Meaning: Briefly, concisely.

Cut to the chase

Meaning: To get directly to the point.

Module 6: Grammar — Formal Connectors

Overview: Formal connectors like Furthermore, However, Nevertheless, Consequently, In addition, Moreover elevate the tone of business writing.

Informal: But we can't do it.
Formal: However, we are unable to proceed.

Informal: Also, we need more time.
Formal: Furthermore, additional time is required.

Module 10: Reading Practice

The Anatomy of a Perfect Professional Email

A perfect professional email is concise, clear, and action-oriented. It begins with a strong subject line that immediately informs the recipient of the email's purpose. The salutation should be appropriate for the relationship level. The body must quickly address the core purpose, using formal language and connectors to ensure professional tone. Slang and informal phrasing should be avoided entirely. Conclude with a clear call to action and a professional sign-off.

Module 13: HR Interview Coaching

Question: "How do you manage your inbox and professional communications?"

Bad

I just reply when I see them.

Good

I check my email twice a day and prioritize the important ones.

Excellent

I use a system to filter and categorize emails. I allocate specific time blocks for deep work where I mute notifications, and I dedicate specific times to clear my inbox efficiently so I never miss a critical message.

Module 18: Common Indian English Mistakes

Wrong: Please do the needful.
Correct: Please process this request. / Please take the necessary action.
Explanation: 'Do the needful' is an archaic British phrase highly overused in India. It sounds very outdated and vague globally.
Wrong: Kindly revert.
Correct: Please reply. / Please get back to me.
Explanation: 'Revert' means to return to a previous state, not to reply to an email.
Wrong: I am waiting for your reply.
Correct: I look forward to your reply. / I await your response.
Explanation: 'Waiting for' sounds demanding or impatient in a formal email.
Wrong: Prepone the meeting.
Correct: Advance the meeting. / Bring the meeting forward.
Explanation: 'Prepone' is an Indianism not recognized in global English.
Wrong: Attachments are enclosed herewith.
Correct: Please find the attachments below. / I have attached...
Explanation: Highly archaic and legally stiff language.
Wrong: As per my knowledge...
Correct: To my knowledge... / As far as I know...
Explanation: Direct translation error.
Wrong: I will intimate you.
Correct: I will inform you. / I will notify you.
Explanation: 'Intimate' as a verb is extremely rare and sounds awkward globally.
Wrong: Give me a revert back.
Correct: Please get back to me.
Explanation: Double redundancy and incorrect usage of 'revert'.
Wrong: Myself John...
Correct: I am John... / My name is John...
Explanation: Grammatically incorrect reflexive pronoun usage.
Wrong: Discuss about the project.
Correct: Discuss the project.
Explanation: 'Discuss' already means 'talk about', so 'about' is redundant.

Module 6: Grammar — Formal Connectors

Overview: Formal connectors like Furthermore, However, Nevertheless, Consequently, In addition, Moreover elevate the tone of business writing.

Informal: But we can't do it.
Formal: However, we are unable to proceed.

Informal: Also, we need more time.
Formal: Furthermore, additional time is required.

Module 10: Reading Practice

The Anatomy of a Perfect Professional Email

A perfect professional email is concise, clear, and action-oriented. It begins with a strong subject line that immediately informs the recipient of the email's purpose. The salutation should be appropriate for the relationship level. The body must quickly address the core purpose, using formal language and connectors to ensure professional tone. Slang and informal phrasing should be avoided entirely. Conclude with a clear call to action and a professional sign-off.

Module 18: Common Indian English Mistakes

Wrong: Please do the needful.
Correct: Please process this request. / Please take the necessary action.
Explanation: 'Do the needful' is an archaic British phrase highly overused in India. It sounds very outdated and vague globally.
Wrong: Kindly revert.
Correct: Please reply. / Please get back to me.
Explanation: 'Revert' means to return to a previous state, not to reply to an email.
Wrong: I am waiting for your reply.
Correct: I look forward to your reply. / I await your response.
Explanation: 'Waiting for' sounds demanding or impatient in a formal email.
Wrong: Prepone the meeting.
Correct: Advance the meeting. / Bring the meeting forward.
Explanation: 'Prepone' is an Indianism not recognized in global English.
Wrong: Attachments are enclosed herewith.
Correct: Please find the attachments below. / I have attached...
Explanation: Highly archaic and legally stiff language.
Wrong: As per my knowledge...
Correct: To my knowledge... / As far as I know...
Explanation: Direct translation error.
Wrong: I will intimate you.
Correct: I will inform you. / I will notify you.
Explanation: 'Intimate' as a verb is extremely rare and sounds awkward globally.
Wrong: Give me a revert back.
Correct: Please get back to me.
Explanation: Double redundancy and incorrect usage of 'revert'.
Wrong: Myself John...
Correct: I am John... / My name is John...
Explanation: Grammatically incorrect reflexive pronoun usage.
Wrong: Discuss about the project.
Correct: Discuss the project.
Explanation: 'Discuss' already means 'talk about', so 'about' is redundant.
