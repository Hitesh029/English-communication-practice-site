DAY 9 OF 30

Navigating Sprint Meetings & Daily Standups

Tier: Intermediate | Grammar: Future Tenses

Module 1: Daily Motivation

"Agility in communication is as important as agility in code."

Welcome to Day 9! Today is all about Agile ceremonies and effectively communicating your progress, blockers, and future plans in daily standups and sprint meetings.

Module 2: Warm-up Conversation

Answer the following questions to warm up.

1. Have you ever participated in a daily standup?

2. What do you say in a standup?

Module 3: Vocabulary

Sprint

Definition: A short, time-boxed period when a scrum team works to complete a set amount of work.

Standup

Definition: A short daily meeting for the team to sync up on progress.

Retrospective

Definition: A meeting held after a sprint to discuss what went well and what didn't.

Burndown

Definition: A chart showing the amount of work remaining versus time.

Velocity

Definition: A measure of the amount of work a team can tackle during a single sprint.

Epic

Definition: A large body of work that can be broken down into specific tasks.

Backlog

Definition: A prioritized list of work for the development team.

Blockers

Definition: Issues that prevent progress on a task.

Deliverable

Definition: A product or service produced as a result of a project.

Capacity

Definition: The amount of work a team can take on in a sprint.

Estimation

Definition: The process of assigning an effort value to a task.

Grooming

Definition: The process of adding detail, estimates, and order to items in the backlog.

Planning

Definition: The meeting where the team decides what to complete in the upcoming sprint.

Review

Definition: A meeting at the end of the sprint to show the work accomplished.

Demo

Definition: A demonstration of the working software.

Release

Definition: A deployment of the software to production.

Increment

Definition: The sum of all backlog items completed during a sprint.

Scrum

Definition: An agile framework for managing complex knowledge work.

Kanban

Definition: A visual method for managing work as it moves through a process.

Waterfall

Definition: A linear, sequential approach to software design and systems development.

Milestone

Definition: A significant point or event in a project.

Deadline

Definition: The latest time or date by which something should be completed.

Dependency

Definition: A relationship where one task relies on the completion of another.

Refinement

Definition: The act of clarifying and updating the backlog.

Acceptance

Definition: The criteria that a system or component must satisfy to be accepted by a user or customer.

Module 4: Phrasal Verbs

Stand up for

Meaning: To defend or support a person or idea.

Wrap up

Meaning: To conclude or finish something.

Pick up slack

Meaning: To do the work that someone else has stopped doing.

Sign off on

Meaning: To officially approve.

Carry over

Meaning: To transfer a task to the next sprint.

Block out

Meaning: To reserve time on a calendar.

Schedule in

Meaning: To plan an activity for a specific time.

Build in

Meaning: To incorporate something as a permanent feature.

Flag up

Meaning: To draw attention to something, especially a problem.

Check off

Meaning: To mark an item on a list as complete.

Module 5: Idioms

Move the goalposts

Meaning: To change the rules or criteria while a process is ongoing.

Drop the ball

Meaning: To make a mistake or fail to do something expected.

In the pipeline

Meaning: In the process of being planned or developed.

On track

Meaning: Progressing as planned.

Behind schedule

Meaning: Not progressing as fast as planned.

Light at the end of the tunnel

Meaning: Signs of improvement in a difficult situation.

Up and running

Meaning: Operating properly.

Ahead of the curve

Meaning: Better or more advanced than others.

Breaking ground

Meaning: Starting a new project.

Keep the momentum

Meaning: Maintain progress.

Module 6: Grammar — Future Tenses (will/going to/Present Continuous)

Overview: We use 'will' for spontaneous decisions, 'going to' for planned intentions, and Present Continuous for fixed arrangements (like meetings).

Will: "I will fix that bug right now." (Just decided)
Going to: "I am going to work on the database next." (Planned intention)
Present Cont: "We are deploying the release at 4 PM." (Fixed arrangement with schedule)

Module 7: Speaking Practice

1. What did you work on yesterday?

2. What will you work on today?

3. Do you have any blockers?

4. Explain a blocker clearly.

Module 8: Role-Play Conversations

Role-Play 1: The Daily Standup Update

Scrum Master: What's your update for today?
You: Yesterday I finished the API. Today I am going to write the tests. I have no blockers.

Role-Play 2: Reporting a Blocker

You: I'm blocked on the frontend integration because the design isn't ready.
Product Owner: Okay, let's sync up with design after standup.

Module 10: Reading Practice

How High-Performing Software Teams Run Sprint Ceremonies

Sprint ceremonies provide the cadence for agile teams. Daily standups should be short—no more than 15 minutes. Participants answer three core questions: what they did yesterday, what they will do today, and if they have any blockers. Standups are not the place for deep technical problem-solving; such discussions are moved to a "parking lot" to be addressed afterward.

Module 12: Writing Practice

Task: Write a sprint retrospective summary.

Model answer: In this sprint, we successfully delivered the authentication module. What went well: team collaboration. What could be improved: code review turnaround time. Action items: Set soft limits on PR reviews...

Module 13: HR Interview Coaching

Question: "How do you handle sprint planning when requirements keep changing?"

Bad

I complain because the scope keeps changing.

Good

I adapt to the changes and try to do my best to complete them.

Excellent

I communicate with the product owner to understand the priority of the changes, re-estimate the effort required, and negotiate what can be moved to the backlog to accommodate the new scope without burning out the team.

Module 18: Common Indian English Mistakes

Wrong: I will be completing this today itself.
Correct: I will complete this today.
Explanation: 'Today itself' is an Indianism. Standard English simply uses 'today'.
Wrong: I am having a blocker.
Correct: I have a blocker. / I am blocked.
Explanation: 'Having' is not typically used for possession or states in this context.
Wrong: The deployment is preponed.
Correct: The deployment has been moved forward.
Explanation: 'Prepone' is not used globally.

Module 6: Grammar — Future Tenses (will/going to/Present Continuous)

Overview: We use 'will' for spontaneous decisions, 'going to' for planned intentions, and Present Continuous for fixed arrangements (like meetings).

Will: "I will fix that bug right now." (Just decided)
Going to: "I am going to work on the database next." (Planned intention)
Present Cont: "We are deploying the release at 4 PM." (Fixed arrangement with schedule)

Module 10: Reading Practice

How High-Performing Software Teams Run Sprint Ceremonies

Sprint ceremonies provide the cadence for agile teams. Daily standups should be short—no more than 15 minutes. Participants answer three core questions: what they did yesterday, what they will do today, and if they have any blockers. Standups are not the place for deep technical problem-solving; such discussions are moved to a "parking lot" to be addressed afterward.

Module 18: Common Indian English Mistakes

Wrong: I will be completing this today itself.
Correct: I will complete this today.
Explanation: 'Today itself' is an Indianism. Standard English simply uses 'today'.
Wrong: I am having a blocker.
Correct: I have a blocker. / I am blocked.
Explanation: 'Having' is not typically used for possession or states in this context.
Wrong: The deployment is preponed.
Correct: The deployment has been moved forward.
Explanation: 'Prepone' is not used globally.
