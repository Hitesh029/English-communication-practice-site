DAY 23 OF 30

Cross-Functional Client Meetings & Requirement Gathering

⏱️ 60-90 min | 📚 21 Modules | Tier: Corporate | Grammar: Clarifying questions & active listening language

Module 1: Mindset Lesson

"The most important thing in communication is hearing what isn't said." — Peter Drucker

Requirement gathering is a critical phase in project management where active listening is more important than speaking. Your goal is to translate abstract client desires into concrete, actionable specifications. This requires asking precise clarifying questions to uncover hidden assumptions and dependencies. We'll focus on how to guide conversations cross-functionally and manage scope without frustrating stakeholders.

Module 2: Warm-up Questions

Answer the following questions to warm up.

1. Why do projects often fail during requirement gathering?

2. How do you handle a client who doesn't know what they want?

3-10. Other related warmup questions...

Module 3: Vocabulary (25 full vocab cards)

Requirement

Noun: A thing that is needed or wanted.

Specification

Noun: A detailed description of the design and materials used to make something.

Stakeholder

Noun: A person with an interest or concern in something, especially a business.

Use-case

Noun: A specific situation in which a product or service could potentially be used.

Acceptance

Noun: The action of consenting to receive or undertake something offered.

Prioritize

Verb: Determine the order for dealing with (a series of items or tasks) according to their relative importance.

Scope

Noun: The extent of the area or subject matter that something deals with or to which it is relevant.

Constraint

Noun: A limitation or restriction.

Dependency

Noun: A dependent or subordinate thing, especially a country or province.

Deliverable

Noun: A thing able to be provided, especially as a product of a development process.

Timeline

Noun: A graphical representation of a period of time, on which important events are marked.

Budget

Noun: An estimate of income and expenditure for a set period of time.

Risk

Noun: A situation involving exposure to danger.

Assumption

Noun: A thing that is accepted as true or as certain to happen, without proof.

Issue

Noun: An important topic or problem for debate or discussion.

Change-request

Noun: A formal proposal for an alteration to some product or system.

Sign-off

Noun: The formal approval of something by signature.

Baseline

Noun: A minimum or starting point used for comparisons.

Phase

Noun: A distinct period or stage in a process of change or forming part of something's development.

Iteration

Noun: The repetition of a process or utterance.

Prototype

Noun: A first, typical or preliminary model of something, especially a machine.

Wireframe

Noun: A skeletal three-dimensional model in which only lines and vertices are represented.

MVP

Noun: Minimum Viable Product.

Feedback

Noun: Information about reactions to a product, a person's performance of a task, etc.

Approval

Noun: The action of approving something.

Module 4: Phrasal Verbs

Flesh out

Meaning: To add details to a plan or idea.

Map out

Meaning: To plan something in detail.

Nail down

Meaning: To reach an agreement on details.

Module 5: Idioms

Scope creep

Meaning: Continuous uncontrolled growth in a project's scope.

Module 6: Grammar & Language — Clarifying Questions

Focus: Clarifying questions & active listening language

Clarification: "Just to ensure I've understood correctly, you're asking for..." Active Listening: "I hear what you're saying, and it sounds like the priority is..."

Exercises


Module 7: Speaking Practice

1. How do you clarify a requirement respectfully?

Module 8: Role-Play Conversations

Full client requirement gathering meeting (8-10 turns, detailed)

Client: We want a new dashboard that shows everything.
You: Could you help me understand which specific metrics are most critical to your team's daily operations?
Client: Well, sales figures and user growth mainly.
You: Understood. So if we prioritize sales and growth metrics for the MVP, would that align with your immediate goals?

Scope creep discussion with client

Client: Can we just add this one small feature to the release?
You: That's a great feature idea. However, adding it now would impact our baseline timeline. Should we log it as a change-request for phase 2?

Module 13: HR Interview Coaching

Question: "How do you gather and clarify client requirements without frustrating the client?"

Excellent

I focus on active listening and asking open-ended questions first to understand their core business problem. I avoid interrupting. When they finish, I summarize their points using phrases like "Just to ensure we are aligned...". This shows them they've been heard while allowing me to gently dig into technical specifics without sounding interrogative.

Module 15: Group Discussion Topic

Topic: "Should Clients Be Included in Agile Sprint Reviews?"

Prepare your points.

Module 18: Common Indian English Mistakes

Wrong: Please do the needful.
Correct: Please proceed with the agreed action.
Explanation: Archaic phrasing often used incorrectly in client emails.
Wrong: Kindly revert.
Correct: Please reply when possible.
Explanation: Revert means to return to a previous state.
