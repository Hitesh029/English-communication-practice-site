DAY 24 OF 30

Salary Negotiation & Corporate Offer Evaluation

⏱️ 60-90 min | 📚 21 Modules | Tier: Corporate | Grammar: Conditional negotiation language — 'If you could consider...', 'Should you be able to...', 'Given that...'

Module 1: Mindset Lesson

"You don't get what you deserve, you get what you negotiate." — Chester Karrass

Negotiating your salary is one of the most critical communication tasks in your career. It requires a delicate balance of confidence and politeness. By using conditional negotiation language, you frame requests not as demands, but as mutually beneficial agreements. Understand your market value, be prepared to articulate the ROI you bring to the company, and never negotiate against yourself.

Module 2: Warm-up Questions

Answer the following questions to warm up.

1. Have you ever negotiated a salary offer?

2. What is the most intimidating part of salary negotiations?

3. Is it better to give a number first or wait for the recruiter?

4. How do you respond to a lowball offer?

5. What non-salary perks are most important to you?

Module 3: Vocabulary (25 full vocab cards)

Compensation

Noun: Something, typically money, awarded to someone in recognition of loss, suffering, or injury, or for work done.

Package

Noun: The total pay and benefits offered to an employee.

Benefits

Noun: Non-wage compensation provided to employees in addition to their normal wages or salaries.

Stipend

Noun: A fixed regular sum paid as a salary or as expenses to a clergyman, teacher, or public official.

Increment

Noun: An increase or addition, especially one of a series on a fixed scale.

Appraisal

Noun: A formal assessment, typically in an interview, of the performance of an employee over a particular period.

Bonus

Noun: A sum of money added to a person's wages as a reward for good performance.

Equity

Noun: The value of the shares issued by a company.

Stock-options

Noun: A benefit in the form of an option given by a company to an employee to buy stock in the company at a discount or at a stated fixed price.

Fixed

Adj: Predetermined and not subject to or able to be changed.

Variable

Adj: Not consistent or having a fixed pattern; liable to change.

CTC

Noun: Cost to Company; the total salary package of an employee.

In-hand

Noun: The net salary received by an employee after deductions.

Gross

Noun: An amount of money before taxes or other deductions are made.

Net

Noun: An amount of money after taxes or other deductions are made.

Perks

Noun: A benefit to which one is entitled because of one's job.

Insurance

Noun: A practice or arrangement by which a company or government agency provides a guarantee of compensation for specified loss, damage, illness, or death in return for payment of a premium.

Leave

Noun: Time when one has permission to be absent from work or from duty.

Flexibility

Noun: The ability to be easily modified.

Notice-period

Noun: The period of time that an employee must work in their job after they have said that they are leaving.

Counter-offer

Noun: An offer made in response to another.

Market-rate

Noun: The usual price in the open market.

Band

Noun: A range of values or salaries within a system.

Revision

Noun: The action of revising.

Negotiate

Verb: Try to reach an agreement or compromise by discussion with others.

Module 4: Phrasal Verbs

Hold out for

Meaning: To wait until you get what you want.

Give in

Meaning: To cease fighting or arguing; admit defeat.

Module 5: Idioms

Lowball offer

Meaning: An offer that is significantly lower than what is expected.

Module 6: Grammar & Language — Conditional Negotiation

Focus: Conditional negotiation language — 'If you could consider...', 'Should you be able to...', 'Given that...'

Conditional Request: "If you could consider matching my current offer, I would be thrilled to accept." Polite Pushback: "Given that the market rate for this skill set is higher, is there any flexibility in the base salary?"

Exercises


Module 7: Speaking Practice

1. How do you ask for a higher base salary?

Module 8: Role-Play Conversations

Phone call with recruiter (HR calling with offer)

HR: We are thrilled to offer you the position with a base salary of $80k.
You: Thank you so much, I'm very excited about this opportunity. Given my specialized experience in React, I was hoping for something closer to $90k. Is there any flexibility there?

Counter-offer discussion email roleplay

You (Email Draft): Dear HR, Thank you for the generous offer. If you could consider adjusting the sign-on bonus, I would be ready to sign immediately.

Module 13: HR Interview Coaching

3 salary expectation scenarios

Scenario 1: Fresher (0 exp) - "What are your salary expectations?"

Bad

I want whatever you can give me.

Good

I expect the industry standard for freshers.

Excellent

As a fresher, my primary goal is to learn and add value to the organization. However, based on my research of market trends for this specific role, I would expect a compensation package in the range of [X to Y].

Scenario 2: 1-2 years exp - "What are your salary expectations?"

Excellent

Given my past year of experience successfully delivering [Project/Skill], I am looking for a competitive offer that reflects my ability to hit the ground running. I am open to discussing the entire compensation package, but I'm targeting the [X] range for the base salary.

Scenario 3: 3-5 years exp - "What are your salary expectations?"

Excellent

Based on the scope of the responsibilities we've discussed and the specialized domain expertise I bring from my previous 4 years in the industry, my expectation is around the [X] mark. Of course, salary is just one component, and I am very interested in evaluating the overall benefits, equity, and growth opportunities within the company.

Module 15: Group Discussion Topic

Topic: "Pay Transparency in Tech Companies: Pros and Cons"

Prepare arguments for both sides.

Module 18: Common Indian English Mistakes

Wrong: What is my in-hand salary?
Correct: What is the net pay? / What is the take-home salary?
Explanation: 'In-hand' is an Indianism. Globally, use 'net pay' or 'take-home'.
