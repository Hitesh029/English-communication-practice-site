Module 3: Vocabulary (25 words)

Narrative

A spoken or written account of connected events; a story.

Anecdote

A short amusing or interesting story about a real incident.

Metaphor

A figure of speech in which a word or phrase is applied to an object or action to which it is not literally applicable.

Analogy

A comparison between two things, typically for the purpose of explanation.

Vivid

Producing powerful feelings or strong, clear images in the mind.

Compelling

Evoking interest, attention, or admiration in a powerfully irresistible way.

Authentic

Of undisputed origin; genuine.

Vulnerable

Susceptible to physical or emotional attack or harm.

Transparent

Easy to perceive or detect.

Engaging

Charming and attractive.

Inspiring

Having the effect of inspiring someone.

Memorable

Worth remembering or easily remembered.

Impactful

Having a major impact or effect.

Persuasive

Good at persuading someone to do or believe something.

Relatable

Enabling a person to feel that they can relate to someone or something.

Universal

Of, affecting, or done by all people or things in the world.

Emotional

Relating to a person's emotions.

Logical

Of or according to the rules of logic or formal argument.

Credible

Able to be believed; convincing.

Authoritative

Able to be trusted as being accurate or true.

Passionate

Showing or caused by strong feelings or a strong belief.

Confident

Feeling or showing confidence in oneself.

Clear

Easy to perceive, understand, or interpret.

Concise

Giving a lot of information clearly and in a few words.

Powerful

Having great power or strength.

Module 5: Idioms (10)

Leave a lasting impression

Capture the room

Hold the audience

Maintain the audience's interest.

Command attention

Act in a way that makes people listen and watch.

Make a splash

Attract a great deal of attention.

Set the stage

Prepare the background or conditions for something.

Break the mold

Do something completely new and different.

Module 6: Grammar — Narrative Arc

Hook types: Provocative question / Shocking statistic / Vivid scene / Personal anecdote

Building tension: 'But then something unexpected happened...', 'Just when we thought it was resolved...'

Resolution language: 'Ultimately, we discovered...', 'The breakthrough came when...'

Call to action: 'So I challenge you today to...', 'The question is: what will you do with this?'

Example 1: "Imagine a world without internet for a day. That's exactly what happened to us last Tuesday..."

Example 2: "Just when we thought the deployment was successful, the servers crashed..."

Example 3: "Ultimately, we discovered that simple communication was the key to our success."

Mistake: Starting with "Hi, my name is...". Correction: Start with a hook!
Mistake: Rambling without a clear point. Correction: Build tension towards a climax.
Mistake: Ending abruptly. Correction: End with a strong call to action.

Module 13: HR Interview Coaching

HR Q: "Tell me about a defining moment that shaped your professional identity."

Bad (30 words): I got promoted to senior dev because I worked hard.
Good (80 words): When I was a junior, I made a huge mistake that crashed the site. Instead of firing me, my manager helped me fix it. That taught me to be accountable.
Excellent (150 words): The defining moment of my career wasn't a success; it was a spectacular failure. In my second year as an engineer, I accidentally deployed code that took down our primary service during peak hours. My heart sank. I expected to be fired immediately. However, instead of berating me, the VP of Engineering gathered the team, calmly led a post-mortem, and asked, "How did our systems fail to catch this?" That single question shifted my entire perspective. It taught me that great engineering culture is about building resilient systems, not blaming individuals. From that day on, my professional identity shifted from just writing code to championing blameless culture and robust CI/CD pipelines. That failure ultimately made me the leader I am today.

Module 14: Tech Communication — Storytelling Templates

1. Hero's Journey: You faced an impossible bug → [struggle] → breakthrough

2. Problem-Solution: Production was down → team rallied → zero-downtime deployment

3. Before-After-Bridge: Slow API before → optimization process → 5x performance gain

4. Data Story: The metrics looked great but... → deeper analysis revealed → we pivoted

5. Vision Story: Imagine a world where... → our work makes this real → here's the roadmap

Module 18: Indian English Mistakes (10)

Wrong: 'I will now explain you the concept.'
Correct: 'I will now explain the concept to you.'

Correct: 'Reply.'

Correct: 'Advance the meeting.'
Wrong: 'Out of station.'
Correct: 'Out of town.'

Module 6: Grammar — Narrative Arc

Hook types: Provocative question / Shocking statistic / Vivid scene / Personal anecdote

Building tension: 'But then something unexpected happened...', 'Just when we thought it was resolved...'

Resolution language: 'Ultimately, we discovered...', 'The breakthrough came when...'

Call to action: 'So I challenge you today to...', 'The question is: what will you do with this?'

Example 1: "Imagine a world without internet for a day. That's exactly what happened to us last Tuesday..."

Example 2: "Just when we thought the deployment was successful, the servers crashed..."

Example 3: "Ultimately, we discovered that simple communication was the key to our success."

Mistake: Starting with "Hi, my name is...". Correction: Start with a hook!
Mistake: Rambling without a clear point. Correction: Build tension towards a climax.
Mistake: Ending abruptly. Correction: End with a strong call to action.

Module 13: HR Interview Coaching

HR Q: "Tell me about a defining moment that shaped your professional identity."

Bad (30 words): I got promoted to senior dev because I worked hard.
Good (80 words): When I was a junior, I made a huge mistake that crashed the site. Instead of firing me, my manager helped me fix it. That taught me to be accountable.
Excellent (150 words): The defining moment of my career wasn't a success; it was a spectacular failure. In my second year as an engineer, I accidentally deployed code that took down our primary service during peak hours. My heart sank. I expected to be fired immediately. However, instead of berating me, the VP of Engineering gathered the team, calmly led a post-mortem, and asked, "How did our systems fail to catch this?" That single question shifted my entire perspective. It taught me that great engineering culture is about building resilient systems, not blaming individuals. From that day on, my professional identity shifted from just writing code to championing blameless culture and robust CI/CD pipelines. That failure ultimately made me the leader I am today.
