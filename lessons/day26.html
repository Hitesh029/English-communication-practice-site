DAY 26 OF 30

Crisis Communication & Production Downtime Retrospectives

Duration: 60-90 min | 21 Modules | Tier: Corporate | Grammar: Passive for accountability without blame

Module 1: Daily Motivation

"In a crisis, clear communication is just as important as the technical fix."

Module 2: Warm-up Conversation

Module 3: Vocabulary (25 words)

Incident

An event that disrupts normal operation.

Postmortem

Analysis after an incident.

Root-cause

The fundamental reason for the occurrence of a problem.

Mitigation

The action of reducing the severity.

Escalation

An increase in intensity or seriousness.

Triage

The process of determining priority.

Rollback

Reverting to a previous state.

Hotfix

A quick fix to a software bug.

Monitor

Observe and check progress.

Alert

A notification of an issue.

Severity

The fact or condition of being severe.

SLA

Service Level Agreement.

Uptime

Time during which a machine is in operation.

Downtime

Time during which a machine is out of action.

Recovery

Return to a normal state.

Transparency

Being open and honest.

Update

Make something more modern.

Timeline

A chronological sequence of events.

Action-item

A specific task or action to be completed.

Retrospective

Looking back on or dealing with past events.

Learning

Knowledge acquired through experience.

Prevention

The action of stopping something from happening.

Accountability

The fact or condition of being accountable.

Containment

Keeping something harmful under control.

Communication

The imparting or exchanging of information.

Module 4: Phrasal Verbs

Bring down

Module 5: Idioms

Put out fires

Module 6: Grammar — Passive for Accountability

Use passive voice to avoid blame: 'It was determined that...', 'The issue has been identified as...', 'Steps have been taken to...'

Don't say: John broke the build. Say: A bad commit broke the build. (Or better: 'The build was broken by a recent commit.')

Module 7: Speaking Practice

Module 8: Role-Play Conversations

Incident bridge call: (engineering team on a live call)
Customer success manager: calling about outage
CEO briefing: on severity-1 incident
Retrospective facilitation: with blame-free culture
Press/PR response: to major outage (simulated)

Module 9: Pronunciation

Module 10: Full Production Incident Sequence

T+0: Initial Slack/Teams alert message

@here SEV-1: Main API is returning 500s. Investigating.

T+15min: Engineering acknowledgment update

We've identified the spike in errors. Appears related to the DB migration. Rolling back now.

T+30min: Customer-facing status page update

We are currently experiencing degraded performance. Our team is actively working on a resolution.

T+60min: Resolution confirmed message

The issue has been resolved and systems are fully operational.

T+3 days: Full postmortem document

Intro, timeline, root cause, contributing factors, action items.

Module 11: Listening Practice

Module 12: Writing Practice

Module 13: HR Interview Coaching

HR Q: 'Tell me about a production incident you handled. What was your role and what did you do?'

Bad: The server crashed, so I restarted it.
Good: I diagnosed a memory leak and implemented a fix while keeping the team informed.
Excellent: During a SEV-1 outage, I led the triage effort. I first contained the issue by rolling back the deployment to restore service for users. Then, I initiated a root-cause analysis, documented the findings, and established an action item to add better automated testing, preventing this specific failure from recurring.
