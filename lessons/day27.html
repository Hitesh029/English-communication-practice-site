Global Remote Work & Intercultural Communication

Duration: 60-90 min | 21 Modules | Tier: Corporate | Grammar: Inclusive and neutral language

Module 1: Daily Motivation

Working globally means communicating empathetically.

Module 2: Warm-up Conversation

Module 3: Vocabulary (25 words)

Synchronous

Existing or occurring at the same time.

Asynchronous

Not existing or happening at the same time.

Timezone

A region of the globe that observes a uniform standard time.

Virtual

Carried out, accessed, or stored by means of a computer.

Distributed

Shared or spread out.

Inclusive

Not excluding any of the parties or groups involved in something.

Diverse

Showing a great deal of variety.

Collaborative

Produced or conducted by two or more parties working together.

Respectful

Feeling or showing deference and respect.

Empathetic

Showing an ability to understand and share the feelings of another.

Cross-cultural

Relating to different cultures or comparison between them.

Intercultural

Taking place between cultures.

Global

Relating to the whole world.

Remote

Having very little connection with or relationship to.

Hybrid

A thing made by combining two different elements.

Engagement

The fact of being involved with something.

Participation

The action of taking part in something.

Documentation

Material that provides official information or evidence.

Transparency

The condition of being transparent.

Alignment

Arrangement in a straight line, or in correct relative positions.

Accessibility

The quality of being easy to approach, reach, enter, speak with, or use.

Etiquette

The customary code of polite behavior in society or among members of a particular profession.

Sensitivity

The quality or condition of being sensitive.

Belonging

An affinity for a place or situation.

Representation

The description or portrayal of someone or something in a particular way.

Module 4: Phrasal Verbs

Module 5: Idioms

Module 6: Grammar — Inclusive and Neutral Language

Use hedging, cultural sensitivity phrases, avoiding assumptions.

Don't say: 'Hey guys, let's meet tomorrow.'
Say: 'Hi team, let's meet on Tuesday.'

Module 8: Role-Play Conversations

Scenario 3: Inclusive meeting facilitation — drawing out quieter team members
Scenario 4: International client call managing cultural expectations
Scenario 5: Remote team-building conversation and virtual social event planning

Module 9: Pronunciation

Module 10: 10 Cross-cultural communication do's and don'ts

Context Do Don't
Japanese colleagues Do say 'Could you share your thoughts?' Don't say 'Why are you being so quiet?'
US colleagues Do be direct and concise. Don't beat around the bush for too long.
German colleagues Do provide detailed data and documentation. Don't make vague promises without plans.
Indian colleagues Do take time to build relationships. Don't assume 'yes' means 'I agree 100%'.
UK colleagues Do understand understated feedback. Don't take 'not bad' as a negative.
Australian colleagues Do use a relaxed but professional tone. Don't be overly formal or hierarchical.
French colleagues Do expect healthy debate. Don't take disagreement personally.
Chinese colleagues Do give face and praise in public. Don't criticize directly in front of others.
Brazilian colleagues Do allow time for casual chat before meetings. Don't rush straight to business.
Middle Eastern colleagues Do respect religious holidays and prayer times. Don't expect immediate replies during weekends (Fri/Sat).

Module 12: Writing Practice

Module 13: HR Interview Coaching

HR Q: 'How do you maintain communication effectiveness in a globally distributed remote team?'

Bad: I make sure everyone speaks English well.
Good: I try to schedule meetings at times that work for everyone and I write clear emails.
Excellent: I prioritize asynchronous communication by maintaining robust documentation and writing context-rich messages, reducing the need for synchronous meetings across timezones. When we do meet, I ensure the agenda is shared in advance, and I actively solicit input from team members who might be culturally less inclined to speak over others.
