DAY 28 OF 30

Executive Polish: Body Language & Vocal Variety

Duration: 60-90 min | 21 Modules | Tier: Corporate | Grammar: Vocal variety notation

Module 1: Daily Motivation

How you say it is just as important as what you say.

Module 2: Warm-up Conversation

Module 3: Vocabulary (25 words)

Posture

The position in which someone holds their body.

Gesture

A movement of part of the body, especially a hand or the head, to express an idea or meaning.

Eye-contact

The act of looking directly into one another's eyes.

Tone

A musical or vocal sound with reference to its pitch, quality, and strength.

Pitch

The quality of a sound governed by the rate of vibrations producing it; the degree of highness or lowness of a tone.

Pace

The speed at which someone or something moves, or with which something happens or changes.

Volume

Quantity or power of sound; degree of loudness.

Pause

A temporary stop in action or speech.

Projection

The strength of speaking or singing whereby the voice is used loudly and clearly.

Articulation

The formation of clear and distinct sounds in speech.

Resonance

The quality in a sound of being deep, full, and reverberating.

Clarity

The quality of being easy to see or hear; sharpness of image or sound.

Authenticity

The quality of being authentic.

Presence

The impressive manner or appearance of a person.

Command

Authority, especially over armed forces.

Authority

The power or right to give orders, make decisions, and enforce obedience.

Warmth

Enthusiasm, affection, or kindness.

Energy

The strength and vitality required for sustained physical or mental activity.

Focus

The center of interest or activity.

Stillness

The absence of movement or sound.

Openness

Lack of restriction; accessibility.

Confidence

The feeling or belief that one can rely on someone or something; firm trust.

Persuasiveness

The ability to persuade.

Gravitas

Dignity, seriousness, or solemnity of manner.

Impact

A marked effect or influence.

Module 5: Idioms

Module 6: Grammar — Vocal Variety Notation

Annotating scripts with pause (//), emphasis (CAPS), pace (slow/fast).

Standard: The results this quarter have been incredible. We grew by 20 percent. Annotated: The results this quarter // have been INCREDIBLE. (slow) We grew by TWENTY percent. (fast)

Module 8: Role-Play Conversations

Panel interview: handling multiple interviewers with body language
Virtual meeting presence: camera angle, lighting, hand gestures
TED-talk style opener: full stage presence simulation

Module 9: Pronunciation

Module 10: Body Language Checklist

In-Person Interview Virtual/Video Interview
Firm handshake and direct eye contact on introduction. Look directly into the camera lens, not the screen, to simulate eye contact.
Sit fully back in the chair with an open posture. Sit up straight, slightly leaning forward towards the camera.
Keep hands visible on the table, avoid crossing arms. Keep hands visible within the frame when gesturing.
Mirror the interviewer's body language subtly. Ensure lighting is from the front, illuminating your face.
Nod occasionally to show active listening. Nod more visibly, as micro-expressions get lost on video.
Maintain appropriate personal space. Frame yourself from chest up (medium close-up).
Use broad gestures for emphasis when standing. Keep gestures within the frame, avoiding blocking your face.
Walk with purpose and energy when entering. Ensure your background is professional and uncluttered.
Plant feet firmly on the ground when standing or sitting. Avoid swiveling or rocking in your chair.
Smile naturally when appropriate. Smile to convey warmth across the digital barrier.

Module 11: Listening Practice

Module 12: Writing Practice (Vocal Variety)

Script 1: Standard delivery (no markup) "Our new product solves the main issue customers complained about last year." Script 2: Pause (//) and EMPHASIS "Our NEW product // SOLVES the main issue // customers complained about last year." Script 3: Pace and volume "(fast, loud) Our NEW product // (slow, soft) solves the main issue... (fast) customers complained about last year."

Module 13: HR Interview Coaching

HR Q: 'How do you project confidence and executive presence in high-stakes situations?'

Bad: I just try not to be nervous.
Good: I prepare thoroughly and make sure my body language is open and professional.
Excellent: I focus on three things: preparation, posture, and pacing. Thorough preparation gives me internal confidence. I project this externally by maintaining an open posture, planting my feet firmly, and making direct eye contact. Finally, I deliberately slow my pacing and use pauses effectively, which conveys authority and gives the audience time to absorb key points.
